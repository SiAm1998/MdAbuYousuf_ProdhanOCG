Satellite-Based Assessment of Particulate Organic Carbon (POC) in the Bay of Bengal: Spatial and Temporal Dynamics

2023–2024 Shahjalal University of Science and Technology Supervisor: Dr. Subrata Sarker
Remote Sensing SNAP Ocean Color

Overview

Particulate Organic Carbon (POC) is an important component of marine carbon cycling, yet its spatial and seasonal dynamics across the Bay of Bengal remain poorly constrained. Using MODIS-Aqua satellite observations from 2015–2024, I assessed basin-wide POC variability and its relationships with chlorophyll-a and sea surface temperature (SST). Surface POC had an overall median concentration of 60.4 mg m⁻³, ranging from 52.6 to 76.8 mg m⁻³, with the highest seasonal median (69.7 mg m⁻³) during the Northeast monsoon and the lowest (47.8 mg m⁻³) during the pre-monsoon. POC showed a very strong positive relationship with chlorophyll-a, highlighting the influence of biological productivity on carbon dynamics in the Bay of Bengal.

Methodology

  • Data: MODIS-Aqua Level-3 satellite datasets for surface POC, chlorophyll-a (Chl-a), and SST, covering the Bay of Bengal from 2015–2024.
  • Tools: NASA Ocean Color data products, R/RStudio, and ArcGIS for data processing, spatial analysis, visualization, and statistical analysis.
  • Methods: Basin-wide POC distributions were analyzed for annual and seasonal variability and compared with Chl-a and SST. Kruskal–Wallis H-test was used to assess seasonal differences, while correlation and trend analyses were applied to examine relationships among variables and long-term changes in POC.

Results

The classified images from the satellite data represent the BoB surface POC concentrations on an annual timescale between 2015–2024 (Figure 2). The central open-ocean center consists of low POC values all over with most values ranging between 30–90 mg m-3, with extensive areas covered with light blue to pale orange. In contrast, coastal waters, particularly on the northern and northeastern edges near the Ganges–Brahmaputra delta, exhibit considerably higher POC values of 120–500 mg m-3, while hotspot regions are as high as 800–1000 mg m-3, evident from orange to red shading. Extreme values exceeding 1000 mg m-3 were detected but constituted less than <0.5% of all pixels. These rare outliers were removed from the visualization by capping the scale at 1000 mg m-3, ensuring clearer representation of the dominant spatial patterns.

The seasonally classified satellite imagery represents the climatological monthly surface concentration variability of POC over the BoB (Figure 3).

Key Findings

  • Strong seasonal signal: POC peaked at 69.7 mg m⁻³ during the Northeast monsoon and fell to 47.8 mg m⁻³ during the pre-monsoon.
  • Biological connection: POC showed a very strong positive relationship with chlorophyll-a, linking carbon availability closely with biological productivity.
  • Significant variability: Seasonal POC differences were statistically significant (Kruskal–Wallis H = 290.43, p < 0.001), demonstrating clear seasonal control of carbon dynamics across the Bay of Bengal.
