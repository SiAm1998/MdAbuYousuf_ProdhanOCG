Modelling the Fate and Trajectories of Hypothetical Oil Spills Near Chattogram and Mongla Ports, Bangladesh

2024–2025 Shahjalal University of Science and Technology Supervisor: Dr. Subrata Sarker
OpenDrift Python CMEMS

Overview

Oil spills pose a major threat to Bangladesh’s coastal ecosystems, particularly around busy ports such as Chattogram and Mongla. I used the OpenOil module of the OpenDrift framework to simulate hypothetical oil spills under summer and winter monsoon conditions, tracking 10,000 oil particles for seven days using ocean and atmospheric forcing data. The simulations revealed strong seasonal differences in oil transport, coastal stranding, and weathering, with some scenarios showing extensive shoreline exposure while others resulted in prolonged offshore transport. The findings highlight the importance of season-specific oil spill preparedness and response strategies for Bangladesh’s coast.

Methodology

Describe the data sources, models, software, and analytical techniques used in this project. Be specific about versions, parameters, and configurations.

  • Data: High-resolution oceanographic and atmospheric forcing data from Copernicus Marine Environment Monitoring Service (CMEMS), including ocean currents, wind, and wave conditions for 2024.
  • Tools: Python, OpenDrift, and its OpenOil module for Lagrangian oil spill simulation and trajectory analysis.
  • Methods: Scenario-based simulations were conducted for offshore locations near Chattogram and Mongla ports under summer and winter monsoon conditions. Each scenario released 10,000 Medium Crude Oil (API 30) particles and simulated their transport and fate for 7 days, incorporating advection by currents and wind, wave effects, and weathering processes including evaporation, emulsification, and biodegradation.

Results

Simulations revealed strong seasonal variability in oil spill dispersion patterns within the Bay of Bengal, driven primarily by monsoon wind reversals and surface currents.

Chattogram Port Analysis

Under summer monsoon conditions, offshore oil transport near Chattogram is heavily influenced by northward coastal currents and strong southwesterly winds.

Key Findings

  • Season matters: Oil fate varied sharply between summer and winter monsoon conditions, producing very different transport and stranding patterns.
  • High coastal exposure: Some scenarios resulted in near-total shoreline stranding, highlighting significant risks to Bangladesh’s vulnerable coastal zones.
  • Weathering changes the story: Evaporation, emulsification, and biodegradation substantially altered oil persistence, reinforcing the need for season-specific spill response strategies.
